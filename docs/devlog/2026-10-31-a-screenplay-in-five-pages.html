Plainchant Dev Diary: A Screenplay in Five Pages

October 31, 2026

Github Repo

Plainchant's front door now introduces the app by doing what the app does: it is a screenplay. The homepage opens on a title page, turns to five numbered sheets, and ends where a script ends, with FADE OUT. and THE END. The words that used to sit in a hero, a feature grid and a closing banner are still there; they are formatted as action, dialogue, a scene heading and a transition instead.

Reading the Primer by Reading the Script

The four-step primer is written in the elements it teaches. A scene heading shows how to start a scene, a character cue shows capitals, a short speech with a parenthetical shows dialogue, and a transition sends the reader to the export. A production note in the paper's left margin marks each step, the way a marked-up draft would. Nothing has to be memorised before the first line is typed.

The same trick carries the rest of the page. The promise is a line spoken by THE WRITER. The features are characters in the whole draft, each with a parenthetical saying what it is: typing, the library, the outline, the promise. Narration becomes a V.O. scene in an editing room. The page explains the format and demonstrates it at the same time.

Paper on the Desk

The sheets are warm paper on the same black-and-blue dithered desk as the rest of the site. The grey pull-down menu bar, the beveled buttons and the one typeface stay; inside a sheet, the colours are ink. The demonstration is still the centre of the first page: a black Void window holds the typed Fountain lines, and beneath it a small white page with a hard shadow holds the same scene, formatted. It is a page becoming a page.

The margins are not decoration. Each sheet is numbered at the top right, the dialogue is indented to screenplay proportions, and transitions sit at the right margin. On a phone the left margin narrows, the notes move above the scene heading and the indents tighten, but the page never turns into a different document.

The Words Stayed

Nothing was cut in the move. The distraction-free promise, the four-step primer, the writing and safekeeping features, the narration support and the invitation to start are all present, along with the manual, the dev diary and the source. It is still one HTML file and one stylesheet: no script, no new file to download and no request to another site. The typefaces were already bundled, and the page is kept for offline use with the editor behind it.

Where the Project Stands

The writing app is untouched. All 339 unit tests pass, including the structure checks that guard the app's files and its offline copy, and the page was walked through in a browser at desktop, tablet and phone widths.

Next: Read It at a Glance

The useful question now is whether the format helps or gets in the way. A visitor who has never seen Plainchant should still understand the promise and reach the first scene without an explanation; the script should read as a page, not a gimmick.
