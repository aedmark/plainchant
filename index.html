Just Type

Plainchant is for writing the scene and leaving the formatting to the software. Write your script as plain text in The Void and The Canvas (Preview) shows it formatted as a screenplay, in real time. The formatting structure comes intuitively from what you type.

Tell the editor what you are writing with Tab or the bar under the text (Scene, Action, Character…), and Enter moves on to whatever normally comes next.

Your Work

  • Every change is saved automatically, and your last script reopens where you left it.
  • New starts a blank script. Library lists every script you have, and lets you search, rename, duplicate, and delete them.
  • A deleted script waits in Recently Deleted for 30 days before it is truly gone, and you can restore it from there until then.
  • Export offers three things:
    • Download a copy (.fountain) saves your script as a plaintext file named after its title. Screenwriting apps that understand Fountain can import it as a script seamlessly.
    • Download a copy (.fdx) saves it in Final Draft’s format, for anyone who works in Final Draft (notes, sections and synopses do not transfer)
    • Print or save as PDF lays it out as standard screenplay pages and opens your browser's print window; choose Save as PDF there to make a shareable PDF.
  • Video chapters lists a timestamp for each top-level section (# Part one), timed by the words read aloud before it, ready to paste into a YouTube description.
  • For narrated videos and voice-over, write what is said as dialogue (under a cue such as NARRATOR) and what is shown as action.
  • Import adds a .fountain, .md or .txt file as a new script. Nothing you already have is replaced.
  • Real files (in Chrome, Edge and other Chromium browsers; not Firefox): Open a file… in the Library opens a .fountain file and keeps it linked, and Sync with a file… in Export keeps the open script in a .fountain file wherever you choose. Unlike a downloaded copy, the file stays in sync: every save writes it, and changes made to it in another program are loaded when you come back to Plainchant (what you had is kept in Versions). If both changed, a bar above the editor asks which should win the merge.
  • Copy puts the whole script on the clipboard.
  • Colors in the editor show what each line is. They are only hints; the formatted script is what prints.
  • Settings chooses a dark or light look (or your system's), the size of the editor's text, and can turn off the colors, the blank line Enter adds after action, and capitals as you type.
  • No connection needed. Plainchant works offline. Opened from a web address, your browser can also install it as an app: look for Install or Add to Home Screen.
  • View Switching choose what you see: the text alone, held to a comfortable width in the middle; both side by side; or the formatted script alone.
  • Quick edits in the preview: double-click a line of the formatted script (press and hold) and its paragraph opens in a small box, as plain text. Fix what you need; Enter keeps the change, Shift+Enter starts a new line, Esc leaves it as it was, and clicking away keeps it too.
  • Page view shows your script as the pages it prints on: page numbers, where each page breaks, (MORE) and (CONT’D), on US Letter or A4 (whichever you last printed on).
  • Outline lists your sections (# Act One), scenes and synopses (= ...), with the page each scene starts on. Choose one to jump there; type to find one.
  • The page count above the formatted script (3 pages · ~3 min) opens Script stats: pages, screen time (about a minute a page), scenes, words, how much each character speaks, the mix of interior and exterior, day and night scenes, each location, and every scene with its page, its length in eighths of a page and who speaks in it (choose one to go there). It also gives the time to read the script aloud.

Scripts live in this browser, on this device (and in a file of your own, if you link one). Nothing saves to your filesystem without your consent, and your writing is never sent to a server. Clearing the browser’s site data deletes your scripts. Before you do that, be sure to export anything you cannot afford to lose.

Plainchant is music with no ornament: a single line, sung unaccompanied. Plain text works the same way.

A screenplay is a handful of element types. Type them like this:

Element You type Notes
Scene heading INT. KITCHEN - NIGHT Starts with INT., EXT., EST. or I/E, after a blank line. Typed in lowercase it is capitalised for you.
Force a scene heading .SNIPER SCOPE POV A leading . makes any line a scene heading.
Action Rain hits the window. He does not look up. Thunder. Any plain text. A blank line starts a new paragraph.
Force action !SILENCE Nobody moves. A leading ! stops an UPPERCASE line being read as a character.
Character and dialogue JOHN (quietly) I’m home. An UPPERCASE name on its own line after a blank line, with the speech directly under it. A line in parentheses is a parenthetical. Extensions: JOHN (V.O.)
Mixed-case name @McCLANE Yippee-ki-yay. A leading @ makes any line a character.
Dual dialogue BRICK Screw you! STEEL ^ Screw you! ^ after the second name puts the two speeches side by side.
Transition CUT TO: An UPPERCASE line ending in TO:. For any other, start with >: > FADE OUT.
Centered > THE END < Wrap the text in > and <.
Title page Title: BIG FISH Credit: Written by Author: Jane Doe The first lines of the script. Also Source, Draft date and Contact.
Notes He waits. [[check the light]] /* cut this later */ [[ ]] shows as a highlighted note. /* */ hides text completely.
Emphasis *italic* **bold** _underline_ Works inside any line.
Outline ## ACT TWO = Hero loses everything. Sections and synopses organize the script; they are not part of the screenplay itself.
Page break ===
Lyrics ~Willy Wonka! Willy Wonka!

Keyboard

Keys What they do
Tab Cycles the current line through Action → Character → Scene heading → Transition. Inside dialogue, it switches between dialogue and parenthetical. On a blank line it chooses what you are about to type: a character (typed text is capitalized) or a scene heading (INT.  is filled in).
Tab while suggestions show Completes the first suggestion instead. While you type a character name or a scene location, names already used in your script appear in the bar under the text; after a location and a dash (INT. KITCHEN - ) the times of day you use appear, then DAY and NIGHT. Enter never picks one, so what you typed stays what you typed. Esc hides them. With Character chosen on an empty line, the names you use most appear too: tap or click one (there Tab still moves on to the next element).
Shift+Tab The same as Tab for cycling, but backwards.
Enter Moves on to what comes next. After a character or a parenthetical you go straight to the speech; after anything else you get a blank line, so an action line ends its paragraph. A name on its own line after a blank line (one your script already uses, or a new one with capitals, like Detective Ruiz) becomes a character, capitalized, with the speech next; Ctrl/Cmd+Z undoes it.
Shift+Enter A plain line break, to keep writing inside the same element.
Esc, then Tab Moves focus out of the editor. (Tab alone is used for cycling.)
Ctrl/Cmd+Z Undo, including anything Tab or Enter just changed.
F1 or Ctrl/Cmd+/ Opens this help.
Ctrl/Cmd+Shift+1, 2, 3 Editor only, editor and preview, preview only (the three small buttons above the preview do the same). On a phone or tablet, 1 and 3 switch between Write and Preview.
Ctrl/Cmd+Shift+F Focus mode on or off (the Focus button at the end of the bar under the text does the same): everything but the block you are writing is dimmed, and the line you are on stays in the middle of the screen. Scrolling still works.
Ctrl/Cmd+P Prints the screenplay pages on the paper chosen last in Export.

Touch

  • The status bar shows what the current line is. Tap Scene, Action, Character, Paren, Dialogue or Transition to change it, or to choose what a blank line will be.
  • While you type a character name (or a location after INT.), names you have already used take the place of those buttons. Tap one to finish the word.
  • On phones and portrait tablets, Write | Preview at the top switches between the text and the formatted script. Preview opens where you were writing, and tapping a line there takes you back to the Void with the cursor on that line.

A character name shows up as ordinary action

A character needs three things upon introduction to be remembered: an UPPERCASE line, a blank line above it, and the speech directly below. Type the name with capitals (MARA) and press Enter, or press Tab on the blank line first. A name in lowercase is only taken for a character once the script has used it before.

An action line in capitals turned into a character

An UPPERCASE line with text right under it looks like a character to every screenplay program. Put ! in front of it, or leave a blank line below it.

My scene heading is not recognized

A scene heading shows in capitals, and the bar under the text says Scene while the cursor is on it. It has to start with INT., EXT., EST. or I/E and have a blank line above it. Or start it with .

Text I pasted is in lowercase and is not formatted

Capitals are added as you type, not to pasted text. Select the line and press Tab to convert it.

Where did my script go?

Open Library, and check Recently deleted if you removed it. Scripts are stored in this browser only, so a different browser, device or a private window shows a different (possibly empty) library. If not there... You may have accidentally deleted your browser cache and indexedDB. Be sure to make frequent backups of important work.

I changed something and want the old text back

Straight away, Ctrl/Cmd+Z undoes it. Later, open Library and choose Versions on the script: Plainchant keeps earlier versions by itself as you write. Compare shows what changed since a version, scene by scene, and can take one scene back (Use the version’s scene, or Put back scene for one you deleted). Go back takes the whole version; Copy makes it a new script.

My PDF has a date or a web address on it

That is the browser’s own header and footer. In its print window, open More settings (or Show details) and turn off Headers and footers.

The page count is not the same as in another program

Every screenwriting program breaks pages a little differently. A page or two either way on a feature is normal.

Blank lines

Extra blank lines are ignored in the formatted script. Use them freely to see your text more clearly.

Esc closes

Export

Files

A snapshot of the script, named after the title. .fountain is plain text: any text editor opens it, and so does any screenwriting app that reads Fountain. .fdx is Final Draft’s own format, for sending to someone who uses Final Draft (most screenwriting apps open it too).

Sync with a file

PDF or paper

Paper

Your browser’s print window opens next. To make a PDF, choose Save as PDF as the printer. If the pages show a date or a web address, turn off Headers and footers there.

Video chapters

Timestamps for a YouTube description, one for each top-level section (# Part one), timed by the words read aloud before it.
